NORTHWIND ANALYTICS GMBH
INTERNAL POLICY IP-014: PERSONAL DATA BREACH AND SECURITY INCIDENT RESPONSE

Document owner: Office of the Data Protection Officer
Version: 4.2
Effective date: 1 March 2024
Review cycle: annual
Classification: Internal — all staff, all contractors, all processors acting on our
instructions

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 This policy sets out how Northwind Analytics GmbH detects, escalates, assesses and
reports a personal data breach or security incident. It applies to every employee,
contractor, intern and temporary worker, and to every third party that processes
personal data on our behalf under a written processing agreement.

1.2 This policy is an internal control. It sits on top of, and never in place of, the
obligations imposed on us by applicable data protection law. Where the law imposes a
requirement this policy does not mention, the legal requirement applies in full. Where
this policy imposes a requirement stricter than the law, this policy applies. Staff must
not treat compliance with this policy as establishing compliance with the law, nor treat
a legal minimum as permission to relax an internal deadline set out here.

1.3 Nothing in this policy authorises any member of staff to decide, on their own
judgement, that an incident does not need to be reported internally. That assessment is
reserved to the Data Protection Officer under section 4.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DEFINITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 "Security incident" means any event that compromises, or that there is reasonable
cause to believe may have compromised, the confidentiality, integrity or availability of
information held by or on behalf of Northwind Analytics GmbH, whether or not personal
data is known to be involved.

2.2 "Personal data breach" means a security incident that leads to the accidental or
unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to,
personal data.

2.3 "Detection" means the first moment at which any member of staff, or any processor
acting on our behalf, becomes aware of facts indicating a security incident. Detection is
not the moment the incident is confirmed, classified, or understood. A suspicion is a
detection.

2.4 "The Data Protection Officer" means the individual appointed under section 3.1,
acting personally or through a formally designated deputy recorded in the incident
register.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. ROLES AND OWNERSHIP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 The company appoints a Data Protection Officer. The appointment, the contact details
and the name of the standing deputy are published on the intranet and are reviewed
annually by the Management Board.

3.2 The Data Protection Officer owns every external notification arising from a personal
data breach. No other function may make an external report, and no other function may
authorise one. This includes Legal, Security Operations, Communications, Sales, and any
member of the Management Board. The reservation is deliberate and absolute: a single
owner is the only way the company can guarantee that what is said externally is
consistent, complete, and made within the deadline that applies to it.

3.3 The Data Protection Officer determines the recipient of any external report, the
contents of that report, and its timing, by reference to the external authority
designated by the national law applicable to the affected processing. Staff must not
attempt to identify the recipient themselves, and must not contact any external body
about an incident, including to make a preliminary or informal enquiry.

3.4 Security Operations owns technical containment and forensic preservation. Legal owns
contractual notification to customers and processors. Communications owns all public and
press statements, which may not be issued before the Data Protection Officer has
confirmed in writing that an external report has been made or is not required.

3.5 Where a processor acting on our behalf detects an incident, the processor must notify
the Office of the Data Protection Officer directly. A processor must not notify any
external body on our behalf, and must not wait for its own internal investigation to
conclude before notifying us.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. INTERNAL ESCALATION — THE FOUR-HOUR RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Any member of staff who detects a security incident must escalate it to the Office of
the Data Protection Officer within four hours of detection. This is the single most
important operational requirement in this policy.

4.2 The four hours run from detection as defined in section 2.3, not from confirmation,
not from classification, and not from the start of the next business day. The four-hour
clock runs continuously, including outside business hours, at weekends and on public
holidays. The Office of the Data Protection Officer maintains a duty rota and an
out-of-hours escalation number for exactly this reason.

4.3 Staff must escalate within four hours even where the facts are incomplete, even where
the incident may turn out to be a false alarm, and even where it is not yet known whether
personal data is involved at all. An escalation containing only the facts known at the
time is compliant. A delayed escalation containing complete facts is not.

4.4 The four-hour internal deadline is an internal control set by this company. It is
deliberately and substantially shorter than any external reporting deadline imposed by
law. The margin is not spare time and must not be treated as such: it is the working
window in which the Data Protection Officer assesses severity, establishes the categories
and approximate number of affected data subjects, coordinates containment with Security
Operations, and prepares an external report that is accurate at the moment it is filed.
Consuming that margin internally is what causes an external deadline to be missed.

4.5 Failure to escalate within four hours is a disciplinary matter, irrespective of
whether the incident is ultimately assessed as a reportable personal data breach and
irrespective of whether any external deadline was in fact missed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ASSESSMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 On receiving an escalation, the Data Protection Officer opens an entry in the
incident register, assigns a reference, and records the detection time reported by the
escalating member of staff.

5.2 The Data Protection Officer assesses whether the security incident constitutes a
personal data breach and, if so, whether it gives rise to an external reporting
obligation, by reference to the statutory tests and deadlines applicable to the
processing concerned. That assessment, including the reasoning where the conclusion is
that no external report is required, is recorded in the incident register.

5.3 The incident register records every security incident escalated under section 4,
including those assessed as not constituting a personal data breach and those assessed as
not externally reportable. The register is retained for five years and is made available
to the Management Board quarterly and to an external authority on request.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. COMMUNICATION TO AFFECTED INDIVIDUALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Where the Data Protection Officer determines that affected individuals must be
informed, the notice is drafted by the Office of the Data Protection Officer and reviewed
by Legal before issue.

6.2 The notice describes the nature of the breach in plain language, the likely
consequences, the measures taken or proposed, and the contact point for further
information. It must not minimise, must not speculate about cause, and must not assign
blame to a named individual.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. EXCEPTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 There is no exception to the four-hour escalation requirement in section 4.1. No
manager, director or board member may waive it, extend it, or instruct a member of staff
to delay an escalation for any reason, including pending legal review, pending customer
communication, or pending confirmation that personal data is involved.

7.2 Any instruction to delay an escalation must itself be escalated to the Data
Protection Officer within four hours.
